Shape Classification Laboratory

Story: A museum has mixed up its shape labels. Can you design a reliable yes/no algorithm to classify every exhibit?

Learning goal

Use observable properties and branching decisions to classify two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional objects.

Success criteria

  1. I can identify defining properties such as sides, vertices, faces and curved surfaces.
  2. I can follow a sequence of yes/no questions to classify a shape.
  3. I can test and improve an algorithm when a shape is incorrectly classified.

Choose an exhibit

Build a decision pathway

Ask one question at a time. Answer based on the exhibit's mathematical properties. The pathway records each choice and explains the result.

    Think, test and improve

    Worked example: A square has four sides and four right angles, so it is also a rectangle. Asking only “Is it a rectangle?” cannot distinguish a square from a non-square rectangle. Add “Are all four sides equal?”

    Misconception: A cube has six square faces, but it is a three-dimensional object, not a square. A square is a two-dimensional shape.

    Challenge: Try every exhibit. Find a question that distinguishes a cylinder from a sphere, and another that distinguishes a cube from a non-cube rectangular prism. Explain why the question is reliable.
