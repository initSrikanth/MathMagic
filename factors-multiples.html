Build Numbers from Factors

Factors are whole numbers that multiply to make a product.

Today’s Learning Goal

Find factor pairs and explain a complete factor set.

Success Criteria: I can…

  • I can build equal-row arrays.
  • I can find both numbers in each factor pair.
  • I can explain when all factors have been found.
KEY IDEA

If 6 × 8 = 48, then 6 and 8 are factors of 48. Factor pairs help us find every factor without missing one.

Find every factor of 24

1 × 24
2 × 12
3 × 8
4 × 6

So the factors of 24 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24.

Recognise Multiples

Multiples are the products made when a number is multiplied by whole numbers.

Today’s Learning Goal

Recognise and explain multiples using equal groups.

Success Criteria: I can…

  • I can build and continue equal-group patterns.
  • I can check a multiple using multiplication.
  • I can explain the factor and multiple relationship.
FACTOR ↔ MULTIPLE

Because 7 × 6 = 42, 7 and 6 are factors of 42, and 42 is a multiple of both 7 and 6.

Multiples form patterns

Multiples of 6:
6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, …

Each step adds another 6. Multiples continue without ending.

Multiples are products formed by multiplying a number by whole numbers.

Adding another equal group produces the next positive multiple.

The list continues without ending; zero is also a multiple, although our counting patterns start at the first positive one.

Picture three groups of six counters.

Make groups of six.

One group gives six, two give twelve, and three give eighteen.

Keep adding six to reach twenty-four, thirty and thirty-six.

Every value in this pattern is a multiple of six because it equals six times a whole number.

Six is a factor of each positive value in the list.

Because seven times six is forty-two, forty-two is a multiple of both seven and six.

The direction of the relationship matters.

Seven and six are factors of forty-two; forty-two is their multiple.

Continuing the sevens pattern from twenty-eight gives thirty-five, forty-two, forty-nine and fifty-six.

Six is a factor of forty-two, and forty-two is a multiple of six.

Reversing those words changes the claim.

Forty-two cannot divide six into a whole number of equal groups.

What comes after forty-eight in the positive multiples of eight?

Add another eight to get fifty-six.

Seven groups of eight make fifty-six.

Multiples come from equal groups and can be continued by repeated addition.

Use multiplication or exact division to check membership.

Factors build a number; multiples are the products you make.

Use Divisibility Rules

A divisibility rule can tell us whether a division will have no remainder.

Today’s Learning Goal

Use divisibility rules and explain why a test passes.

Success Criteria: I can…

  • I can choose the test for the divisor.
  • I can use final digits or digit sums correctly.
  • I can combine the tests for two and three to test six.
USEFUL TESTS
2Last digit is even.
3Digit sum is divisible by 3.
4Last two digits are divisible by 4.
5Last digit is 0 or 5.
6Divisible by both 2 and 3.
9Digit sum is divisible by 9.
10Last digit is 0.

Is 738 divisible by 6?

738 is even → divisible by 2.
7 + 3 + 8 = 18 → divisible by 3.
So 738 is divisible by 6.

Is seven hundred and thirty-eight divisible by six?

A number is divisible by six when it is divisible by both two and three.

Its last digit, eight, is even.

The digit sum is seven plus three plus eight, which is eighteen.

Eighteen is divisible by three.

Both tests pass, so seven hundred and thirty-eight is divisible by six.

Is one thousand two hundred and forty-five divisible by five and by nine?

It ends in five, so the test for five passes.

Its digit sum is twelve.

Twelve is not divisible by nine, so the number is not divisible by nine.

Passing one divisibility test does not imply passing every other test.

An even number is divisible by two, but not always by six.

Fourteen is even, yet its digit sum is five, which is not divisible by three.

To test six, both conditions must pass.

Is three hundred and twelve divisible by four?

The last two digits form twelve.

Twelve is divisible by four, so three hundred and twelve is divisible by four.

Check: four times seventy-eight is three hundred and twelve.

Choose the rule for the divisor and explain each condition.

Six needs both the two and three tests.

Divisibility rules are shortcuts for exact-division decisions, and multiplication can confirm the result.

Follow an Algorithm

An algorithm is a sequence of steps and decisions that can be repeated.

Today’s Learning Goal

Follow an algorithm to find factors systematically.

Success Criteria: I can…

  • I can repeat the test and record steps.
  • I can record pairs only for exact division.
  • I can stop before factor pairs repeat.
FACTOR-FINDING ALGORITHM

1. Start with 1. 2. Test whether the number divides exactly. 3. If yes, record both numbers in the factor pair. 4. Increase the test number. 5. Stop when the factor pairs begin to repeat.

Apply the steps to 36

1 × 36 ✓
2 × 18 ✓
3 × 12 ✓
4 × 9 ✓
5 ✗
6 × 6 ✓

After 6 × 6, the pairs would repeat in reverse, so the search can stop.

Connect and Reason

Use factors, multiples and divisibility together to solve unfamiliar questions.

Today’s Learning Goal

Explain shared factors and shared multiples.

Success Criteria: I can…

  • I can compare factor lists for shared values.
  • I can compare equal-group patterns for shared multiples.
  • I can check and explain a shared relationship.

Shared factors

The factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.
The factors of 30 are 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30.
They share 1, 2, 3 and 6.

Shared multiples

Multiples of 4 include 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, …
Multiples of 6 include 6, 12, 18, 24, …
So 12 and 24 are shared multiples.

Try it

Explain

A student says, “Every multiple of 6 is also a multiple of 3.” Is the student correct? Explain using the factor relationship.
