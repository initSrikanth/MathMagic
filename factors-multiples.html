Build Numbers from Factors

Factors are whole numbers that multiply to make a product.

KEY IDEA

If 6 × 8 = 48, then 6 and 8 are factors of 48. Factor pairs help us find every factor without missing one.

Find every factor of 24

1 × 24
2 × 12
3 × 8
4 × 6

So the factors of 24 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24.

Recognise Multiples

Multiples are the products made when a number is multiplied by whole numbers.

FACTOR ↔ MULTIPLE

Because 7 × 6 = 42, 7 and 6 are factors of 42, and 42 is a multiple of both 7 and 6.

Multiples form patterns

Multiples of 6:
6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, …

Each step adds another 6. Multiples continue without ending.

Use Divisibility Rules

A divisibility rule can tell us whether a division will have no remainder.

USEFUL TESTS
2Last digit is even.
3Digit sum is divisible by 3.
4Last two digits are divisible by 4.
5Last digit is 0 or 5.
6Divisible by both 2 and 3.
9Digit sum is divisible by 9.
10Last digit is 0.

Is 738 divisible by 6?

738 is even → divisible by 2.
7 + 3 + 8 = 18 → divisible by 3.
So 738 is divisible by 6.

Follow an Algorithm

An algorithm is a sequence of steps and decisions that can be repeated.

FACTOR-FINDING ALGORITHM

1. Start with 1. 2. Test whether the number divides exactly. 3. If yes, record both numbers in the factor pair. 4. Increase the test number. 5. Stop when the factor pairs begin to repeat.

Apply the steps to 36

1 × 36 ✓
2 × 18 ✓
3 × 12 ✓
4 × 9 ✓
5 ✗
6 × 6 ✓

After 6 × 6, the pairs would repeat in reverse, so the search can stop.

PAGE 5

Connect and Reason

Use factors, multiples and divisibility together to solve unfamiliar questions.

Shared factors

The factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.
The factors of 30 are 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30.
They share 1, 2, 3 and 6.

Shared multiples

Multiples of 4 include 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, …
Multiples of 6 include 6, 12, 18, 24, …
So 12 and 24 are shared multiples.

Try it
