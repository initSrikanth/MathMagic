PAGE 1 • DECIMAL PLACE VALUE

Read, write and rename decimals

Today’s Learning Goal

Read and explain decimal place value to thousandths.

Success criteria: I can…

  • Name the tenths, hundredths and thousandths places.
  • Explain a decimal using a place-value chart.
  • Identify the value of a digit in a decimal number.

Key idea

Digits to the right of the decimal point represent parts of one whole. The first place is tenths, the second is hundredths, and the third is thousandths.

ones.tenthshundredthsthousandths4.307

So 4.307 means 4 ones, 3 tenths, 0 hundredths and 7 thousandths.

Worked example

What is the value of the 6 in 12.064?

0.06 = 6 hundredths

The 6 is in the hundredths place, so its value is six hundredths.

Try it

What is the value of the 8 in 5.218?

Reason

Why are 3.5 and 3.500 equal? Adding zeros to the right of the final decimal digit does not change the value.

Our learning goal is to read and explain decimal place value to thousandths.

Success criteria. I can name the tenths, hundredths and thousandths places. I can explain a decimal using a place-value chart. I can identify the value of a digit in a decimal number.

To the right of the decimal point come tenths, hundredths and thousandths. Each place is one tenth of the place to its left. A digit gives the number of those units; its position gives their size.

Read four point three zero seven. The four represents four ones, the three represents three tenths, and the seven represents seven thousandths. There are no hundredths.

The zero keeps the seven in the thousandths place. Without that zero, four point three seven would have seven hundredths, a different amount.

Find the value of the six in twelve point zero six four. The six is the second digit after the decimal point.

The six represents six hundredths, or zero point zero six. Name the place before stating the value. It does not represent six tenths or six whole ones.

What is the value of the eight in five point two one eight?

It is eight thousandths, or zero point zero zero eight. The eight is in the third decimal place.

Read the places in order: ones, tenths, hundredths, thousandths. Zeros can hold a place. Adding zeros at the end, as in three point five and three point five zero zero, leaves the value unchanged.

PAGE 2 • COMPARE, ORDER & LOCATE

Use place value to compare decimals

Today’s Learning Goal

Compare, order and locate decimal numbers using place value and number lines.

Success criteria: I can…

  • Compare decimal digits from left to right.
  • Use placeholder zeros to compare equivalent decimal forms.
  • Locate decimals accurately between labelled points on a number line.

Key strategy

Compare from left to right: whole numbers first, then tenths, hundredths and thousandths. You may add placeholder zeros to make the places easier to compare.

4.607 = 4.607    and    4.68 = 4.680

Worked example

4.680 > 4.607

The whole-number and tenths digits match. At the hundredths place, 8 hundredths is greater than 0 hundredths, so 4.680 is greater than 4.607.

1.21.251.3

1.25 lies halfway between 1.2 and 1.3.

Try it

Which is greater: 2.405 or 2.45?

Reason

A student says 0.62 is less than 0.599 because 62 is less than 599. Explain why comparing the digits as whole numbers gives the wrong conclusion.

Success criteria. I can compare decimal digits from left to right. I can use placeholder zeros to compare equivalent decimal forms. I can locate decimals accurately between labelled points on a number line.

Compare from left to right. If the whole numbers match, compare tenths, then hundredths, then thousandths. Trailing zeros can show equal places without changing a value. Decimal digits are not a separate whole number.

Compare four point six eight and four point six zero seven. Rename four point six eight as four point six eight zero. The ones and tenths match.

At the hundredths place, eight is greater than zero. That decides the comparison: four point six eight is greater. The thousandths cannot reverse it.

Now compare zero point six two with zero point five nine nine. Both have zero whole ones. At the tenths place, six is greater than five.

So zero point six two is greater, even though it has fewer written decimal digits. Comparing sixty-two with five hundred and ninety-nine would compare the wrong units.

Which is greater: two point four zero five, or two point four five?

Two point four five equals two point four five zero. The first differing place is the hundredths, so two point four five is greater.

Align equal place values and compare from the largest place. Stop at the first difference. On a number line the greater number is further right; the number of written digits alone does not decide.

PAGE 3 • UNDERSTANDING PERCENT

Percent means “out of 100”

Today’s Learning Goal

Explain percentages as parts of a whole divided into 100 equal parts.

Success criteria: I can…

  • Explain what the percent symbol means.
  • Represent a familiar percentage using a hundred-part model.
  • Compare percentages by thinking about the same whole.

Key idea

The symbol % means “per hundred”. 100% is one complete whole. Percentages let us describe and compare relative size using the same scale of 100.

65% = 65 out of 100

Worked example

A tablet battery is at 75%. This means 75 out of every 100 equal parts of the full battery are represented.

75% is more than half (50%) but less than the complete whole (100%).

Try it

Which is a greater relative amount: 40% or 60%?

Reason

Team A wins 8 of 10 games. Team B wins 75 of 100 games. Represent both results as percentages to decide which team has the greater relative success.

Percent means per hundred. One hundred percent is one complete whole. Fifty percent is half of that whole. Always identify the whole: a percentage describes a share of it, rather than a count by itself.

A battery at seventy-five percent represents seventy-five of one hundred equal parts of full capacity. It is more than half full but less than completely full.

The percentage bar shows the same relative share: three quarters of its complete length. The full bar always stands for the same full battery in this comparison.

Compare forty percent and sixty percent of the same whole. Imagine forty shaded cells and sixty shaded cells on equal hundred-grids.

Sixty percent is the greater share because sixty of the same-sized parts is more than forty. Both shares use a common scale of one hundred.

Which is closer to a complete whole: ninety percent or twenty-five percent?

Ninety percent is only ten of the hundred parts below the whole. Twenty-five percent is seventy-five parts below it. Ninety percent is closer.

Percent means out of one hundred equal parts. Identify the whole, use familiar benchmarks such as fifty and one hundred percent, and compare relative shares on the same scale.

A fraction describes equal parts, a decimal uses place-value parts, and a percentage uses hundredths. Rename a familiar fraction in hundredths to connect all three. Always keep the whole unchanged.

One half of a whole is fifty hundredths. In decimal notation that is zero point five zero, or zero point five.

Fifty hundredths is fifty percent. All three names identify the midpoint between zero and one, so the amount has not changed.

Three quarters is seventy-five hundredths. In decimal notation it is zero point seven five.

Seventy-five hundredths is seventy-five percent. One quarter would be twenty-five hundredths, or zero point two five, or twenty-five percent.

One fifth is how many tenths, and what decimal and percentage does it represent?

One fifth is two tenths, or twenty hundredths. That gives zero point two and twenty percent.

Keep the same whole and connect through equal parts. One half, one quarter, three quarters, one tenth and one fifth are useful benchmarks. Equivalent representations locate the same amount on a number line.

PAGE 5 • APPLY & REASON

Represent and compare relative size

Today’s Learning Goal

Use decimal and percentage representations to compare real-world relative amounts.

Success criteria: I can…

  • Convert familiar decimals and fractions into percentages.
  • Compare amounts using a common whole or a scale of 100.
  • Explain why comparing numbers in different forms can be misleading.

Use a common whole

Percentages make comparisons fair because they describe amounts on the same scale of 100. 100% represents the complete whole. Familiar percentages can also be shown as fractions and decimals.

Worked example

Team A wins 8 out of 10 games. Team B wins 75 out of 100 games.

8 out of 10 = 80 out of 100 = 80%

75 out of 100 = 75%

So Team A has the greater relative success.

Compare different representations

0.5=12=50%

Each representation describes the same relative amount: one half of the whole.

Try it

Which represents the greater relative amount: 0.75 or 60%?

Reason

A student says 25% is greater than 0.5 because 25 is greater than 5. Explain why the representations must first be connected to equivalent forms.

Year 5 boundary: focus on decimal place value, comparison and ordering, number-line representation, common percentages, relative size and familiar fraction–decimal–percentage equivalents. Finding a percentage of a quantity and percentage discounts are developed in Year 6.

Counts alone can be misleading when the totals differ. Compare the share of each total by renaming it on a common scale. Percentages use a scale of one hundred; they do not tell us how many items there were originally.

Team A wins eight out of ten games. Rename the share as eighty out of one hundred, which is eighty percent.

Team B wins seventy-five out of one hundred games, which is seventy-five percent. Eighty percent is greater, so Team A has the greater relative success, although Team B has more wins in total.

Compare a bar that is half shaded with a bar that is zero point four shaded. Use equal complete bars for the wholes.

Half is fifty percent and zero point four is forty percent. Half is the greater share because fifty hundredths is more than forty hundredths.

Which share is greater: three quarters or seventy percent?

Three quarters is seventy-five percent. That is greater than seventy percent by five percentage points.

Identify each whole, rename shares on a common scale, and compare relative size. A greater percentage does not necessarily mean a greater count when the totals differ. Explain which comparison the question asks for.
