PAGE 1 • DECIMAL PLACE VALUE

Read, write and rename decimals

Key idea

Digits to the right of the decimal point represent parts of one whole. The first place is tenths, the second is hundredths, and the third is thousandths.

ones.tenthshundredthsthousandths4.307

So 4.307 means 4 ones, 3 tenths, 0 hundredths and 7 thousandths.

Worked example

What is the value of the 6 in 12.064?

0.06 = 6 hundredths

The 6 is in the hundredths place, so its value is six hundredths.

Try it

What is the value of the 8 in 5.218?

Reason

Why are 3.5 and 3.500 equal? Adding zeros to the right of the final decimal digit does not change the value.

PAGE 2 • COMPARE, ORDER & LOCATE

Use place value to compare decimals

Key strategy

Compare from left to right: whole numbers first, then tenths, hundredths and thousandths. You may add placeholder zeros to make the places easier to compare.

4.607 = 4.607    and    4.68 = 4.680

Worked example

4.680 > 4.607

The whole-number and tenths digits match. At the hundredths place, 8 hundredths is greater than 0 hundredths, so 4.680 is greater than 4.607.

1.21.251.3

1.25 lies halfway between 1.2 and 1.3.

Try it

Which is greater: 2.405 or 2.45?

Reason

A student says 0.62 is less than 0.599 because 62 is less than 599. Explain why comparing the digits as whole numbers gives the wrong conclusion.

PAGE 3 • UNDERSTANDING PERCENT

Percent means “out of 100”

Key idea

The symbol % means “per hundred”. 100% is one complete whole. Percentages let us describe and compare relative size using the same scale of 100.

65% = 65 out of 100

Worked example

A tablet battery is at 75%. This means 75 out of every 100 equal parts of the full battery are represented.

75% is more than half (50%) but less than the complete whole (100%).

Try it

Which is a greater relative amount: 40% or 60%?

Reason

Team A wins 8 of 10 games. Team B wins 75 of 100 games. Represent both results as percentages to decide which team has the greater relative success.

PAGE 5 • APPLY & REASON

Use familiar percentages in real situations

Partition the whole

Familiar percentages can be reasoned from known fractions. For example, 10% is one tenth and 50% is one half. These relationships help us reason about quantities without a formal percentage formula.

Worked example

There are 40 counters. What is 60% of the collection?

10% of 40 = 4

50% of 40 = 20

60% = 50% + 10%, so 20 + 4 = 24

Compare relative size

Class A has completed 50% of a challenge. Class B has completed 75%. Even if the classes contain different numbers of students, 75% represents the greater relative amount.

Try it

A collection has 80 counters. Use one quarter to find 25% of the collection.
