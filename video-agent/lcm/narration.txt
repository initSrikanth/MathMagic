The lowest common multiple is the smallest positive number that appears in both numbers' times tables. We call it the L C M.

Let's find it for four and six. Multiples of four are four, eight, twelve, sixteen, and so on.

Multiples of six are six, twelve, eighteen, and so on.

Twelve is the first positive number in both lists. So twelve is the lowest common multiple of four and six.

That helps us add one quarter and one sixth. We need equal-sized parts. Twelve is a useful common denominator because both four and six divide into twelve.

Multiply both numbers in one quarter by three. One quarter equals three twelfths. Multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same number keeps the fraction's value unchanged.

For one sixth, multiply the numerator and denominator by two. One sixth equals two twelfths. These fractions now use the same-sized parts.

Add three twelfths and two twelfths. That makes five twelfths. Keep the denominator twelve. The answer is already in simplest form.

Remember: find the shared multiples, choose the smallest positive one, rename, then calculate. Any common denominator can work, but the lowest common multiple usually keeps the numbers smaller.
