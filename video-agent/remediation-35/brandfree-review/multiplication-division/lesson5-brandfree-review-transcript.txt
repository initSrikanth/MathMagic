Can multiplication help you solve a division, or uncover a hidden number? Today we are connecting inverse operations, estimates and real situations. We will identify equal groups, find a missing value, and check whether an answer has the right size.

Our learning goal is to use inverse operations, estimates and equal-group models.

Success criteria. I can find a missing factor using an inverse relationship. I can estimate before finding an exact total. I can explain an answer with its meaning and unit.

Multiplication and division describe the same equal-group relationship. If groups times group size gives the total, dividing the total by one known factor finds the other. An estimate provides a separate size check. Three equal groups of four make twelve. Sharing twelve into three equal groups gives four in each.

A missing number times twenty-four equals one thousand six hundred and eighty. Divide the total by twenty-four to find the missing number.

Seventy groups of twenty-four make one thousand six hundred and eighty. Substitute seventy into the original sentence to verify the relationship.

A shop has twenty-one boxes with three hundred and ninety-eight pencils in each. Estimate using twenty boxes of four hundred: about eight thousand pencils.

The exact calculation is three hundred and ninety-eight times twenty plus one more group. Seven thousand nine hundred and sixty plus three hundred and ninety-eight gives eight thousand three hundred and fifty-eight pencils.

An estimate is a useful check, but do not use eight thousand as the exact total. Keep twenty-one groups of three hundred and ninety-eight for the real calculation. An inverse check uses the exact values, not the rounded ones.

A missing number times eighteen equals one thousand four hundred and forty. What is it?

Eighty times eighteen equals one thousand four hundred and forty, so the missing number is eighty.
