When a multiplier has two digits, does the tens digit count as ones? Today we are multiplying by two-digit numbers. We will separate tens and ones, calculate two partial products, and connect them to the complete multiplication.

Our learning goal is to multiply by two-digit numbers using tens and ones.

Success criteria. I can split a multiplier into tens and ones. I can label and calculate both partial products. I can add the products and check their size.

Thirteen means ten plus three. Multiplying by thirteen means taking ten groups and three more groups. Missing the tens value is a common error; label each partial product before adding. In a small picture, imagine two rows of thirteen counters. Each row has ten and three more. Both parts still belong in the answer.

Calculate two hundred and forty-six times thirteen. First take ten groups: two hundred and forty-six times ten is two thousand four hundred and sixty.

Then take three groups: two hundred and forty-six times three is seven hundred and thirty-eight. Add the partial products to get three thousand one hundred and ninety-eight.

Now calculate three hundred and fifteen times twenty-four. Twenty-four is twenty plus four. Three hundred and fifteen times twenty is six thousand three hundred.

Four groups make one thousand two hundred and sixty. Combining twenty groups and four groups gives seven thousand five hundred and sixty.

The two in twenty-four means twenty, not two. Multiplying by two instead of twenty misses a place value. Label twenty groups and four groups, then combine the correct partial products.

What is one hundred and two times twelve?

Ten groups make one thousand and twenty, and two groups make two hundred and four. The total is one thousand two hundred and twenty-four.
