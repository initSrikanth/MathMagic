A playground has a corner cut out for a tree. Can we still work out how many paving tiles it needs? An irregular outline does not stop us measuring. Let us turn the tricky shape into familiar rectangles.

Our learning goal is to solve practical problems with irregular shapes.

Success criteria. I can split a shape or subtract a missing rectangle. I can check every outside length for perimeter. I can compare designs using the required measure.

Build an L shape with square tiles, then slide a pencil along possible dividing lines. You can split its area into non-overlapping rectangles, or start with a large rectangle and subtract the missing part. Both methods must cover exactly the same surface.

An L-shaped space fits inside an eight-metre by six-metre rectangle. A three-metre by two-metre corner is missing. Predict whether its area is greater or smaller than forty-eight square metres.

Subtract the missing six square metres from forty-eight. The L-shaped area is forty-two square metres. As a check, split it into an eight-by-four rectangle and a five-by-two rectangle. Thirty-two plus ten also gives forty-two.

A larger design fits inside a ten-metre by eight-metre rectangle, with a four-by-three corner missing. Plan the area calculation first. Keep the border measurement separate from the surface measurement.

The usable area is sixty-eight square metres. For its border, trace ten, five, four, three, six and eight metres. That perimeter is thirty-six metres. Different questions about the same design need different measures.

Using the full eight-by-six rectangle would include the tree corner. That overcounts six square metres. Subtract the missing rectangle, or add two rectangles that cover only the actual playground.

A six-by-five rectangle has a two-by-two corner removed. What area remains? Look at the model, and keep your prediction in mind.

Thirty minus four leaves twenty-six square units. The missing corner is not part of the surface, so subtract it once, rather than including the full bounding rectangle.

Choose the measure the problem needs. For area, split or subtract rectangles; for perimeter, trace every outside edge. Draw a garden on squared paper and explain a paving plan and a fencing plan. Check that your answers describe the actual design. Reflect on which model helped you and why.
