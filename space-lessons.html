LESSON 1

3D objects and their properties

A museum needs labels for its collection of solids.

Learning goal

Understand and apply 3d objects and their properties in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can identify faces, edges and vertices
  2. distinguish flat faces from curved surfaces
  3. justify how I recognise a solid.

Explore and learn

A cube has 6 square faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. A triangular prism has 2 triangular and 3 rectangular faces, 9 edges and 6 vertices.

Common misconception

A cylinder has curved surface and two flat circular faces; do not call its circular boundaries vertices.

Your challenge

A box has six faces. How could you prove it is a rectangular prism rather than a cube?

Show a helpful strategy

Count faces by shape and compare edge lengths.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 2

Explore nets

A designer wants to make a gift box from one sheet of cardboard.

Learning goal

Understand and apply explore nets in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can connect a 3D object to its 2D net
  2. predict which faces meet
  3. explain why a proposed net works or fails.

Explore and learn

A cube net has six squares joined edge to edge, but not every arrangement of six squares folds into a cube. A square-based pyramid has a square base and four triangular side faces.

Common misconception

Six squares in a 2-by-3 rectangle overlap when folded into a cube: counting faces alone does not guarantee a valid net.

Your challenge

Draw a cross of six squares. Which squares become opposite faces when folded?

Show a helpful strategy

Label faces and trace which edges will touch.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 3

Design and test nets

A packaging company wants a carton that closes without overlapping faces.

Learning goal

Understand and apply design and test nets in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can sketch a net for a chosen prism
  2. check face count and dimensions
  3. revise a net after predicting overlaps.

Explore and learn

A rectangular prism with dimensions 2, 3 and 4 units needs two 2×3 faces, two 2×4 faces and two 3×4 faces. Matching edge lengths must meet when folded.

Common misconception

A net is not correct just because it contains the right number of rectangles; the arrangement and adjoining edges matter.

Your challenge

Sketch a net for a 2×3×4 box. Mark each pair of matching opposite faces.

Show a helpful strategy

Compare the edge lengths along every fold.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 4

Coordinates and position

A rescue robot needs an exact location on a map.

Learning goal

Understand and apply coordinates and position in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can identify the origin
  2. read horizontal then vertical coordinates
  3. locate a point in the first quadrant.

Explore and learn

Start at (0,0). The point (4,2) means travel four units horizontally to the right and two units up. Coordinates describe intersections, not grid squares.

Common misconception

(2,4) and (4,2) are different points; the order matters.

Your challenge

Plot (1,5), (5,1), and (3,3). Which lies on the line where both coordinates are equal?

Show a helpful strategy

Say 'across, then up' for each ordered pair.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 5

Map routes and directions

A courier must visit three locations without getting lost.

Learning goal

Understand and apply map routes and directions in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can follow a sequence of coordinate moves
  2. write precise directions
  3. check the final location.

Explore and learn

Starting at (2,1), moving three units right and two units up ends at (5,3). Each move changes only the relevant coordinate.

Common misconception

A move of 'up 2' does not change the first coordinate.

Your challenge

Start at (1,2). Move right 4, up 3, left 2. Where do you finish?

Show a helpful strategy

Track the horizontal and vertical coordinates separately.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 6

Translations

A game character slides across a tiled board.

Learning goal

Understand and apply translations in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can translate a shape
  2. describe the movement using directions and distances
  3. identify properties that stay unchanged.

Explore and learn

A translation of 3 right and 2 up moves every vertex by the same displacement. Side lengths, angles and orientation remain unchanged.

Common misconception

Sliding a triangle is not rotating it; every point must move the same distance in the same direction.

Your challenge

Translate vertices (1,1), (3,1), (2,3) by 2 right and 1 up.

Show a helpful strategy

Apply the same movement to each vertex.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 7

Reflections

An artist wants a perfect mirror-image logo.

Learning goal

Understand and apply reflections in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can reflect a shape in a line
  2. identify corresponding points
  3. explain equal perpendicular distances.

Explore and learn

For the vertical mirror line x=5, the point (3,2) reflects to (7,2). The original and image are each 2 units from the line.

Common misconception

Reflection reverses orientation. Merely moving a shape across a mirror line is not necessarily reflection.

Your challenge

Reflect (2,1) across x=4. What is the image point?

Show a helpful strategy

Measure the horizontal distance to the mirror line.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 8

Rotations and symmetry

A windmill designer wants blades that match after a turn.

Learning goal

Understand and apply rotations and symmetry in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can rotate a shape around a specified centre
  2. recognise quarter and half turns
  3. identify rotational symmetry.

Explore and learn

A quarter-turn is 90°. A square matches itself after a 90° turn around its centre; a non-square rectangle first matches after 180°.

Common misconception

The centre of rotation matters; turning around a corner differs from turning around the centre.

Your challenge

Which turns less than 360° map a square onto itself? Explain.

Show a helpful strategy

Imagine rotating tracing paper around the exact centre.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 9

Classification algorithms

A scientist needs a reliable way to sort shapes.

Learning goal

Understand and apply classification algorithms in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can choose observable properties
  2. build yes/no decisions
  3. test and improve a classification algorithm.

Explore and learn

Ask 'Does it have a curved surface?' to distinguish some solids. For polygons, ask 'Does it have exactly four sides?' then 'Are all four sides equal?' Each question must have a clear yes/no answer.

Common misconception

'Does it look like a square?' is vague. A square is also a rectangle, so categories may overlap.

Your challenge

Write a decision tree that distinguishes triangles, quadrilaterals and pentagons by side count.

Show a helpful strategy

Choose properties that can be checked rather than appearances.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?

LESSON 10

Integrated spatial design challenge

A park planner must build a model, map paths and design a repeating logo.

Learning goal

Understand and apply integrated spatial design challenge in real problems.

Success criteria

  1. I can select a valid net
  2. communicate coordinate routes
  3. use transformations and justify classifications.

Explore and learn

Create a small kiosk from a rectangular-prism net, plot its position on a first-quadrant map, translate a garden motif and classify its shapes with a branching algorithm.

Common misconception

A convincing diagram is not enough: coordinates, folding edges and transformations must all be mathematically consistent.

Your challenge

Design a 10×10 park grid with three labelled locations, one reflected motif and a kiosk net. Explain each decision.

Show a helpful strategy

Check every coordinate, matching net edge and transformation invariant.

Reflect: What did you learn, and how would you explain it to someone else?
