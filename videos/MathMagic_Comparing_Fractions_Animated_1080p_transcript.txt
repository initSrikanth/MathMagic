Which is bigger: three quarters or one half? Let’s build the amounts and find out.

A fraction starts with equal parts. Here, one whole is divided into four equal pieces.

Shade three pieces. The numerator, three, counts the shaded parts. The denominator, four, counts the equal parts in the whole.

A strip shows the same amount. Three of four equal lengths are shaded. The picture changes, but the value stays the same.

When denominators match, the pieces have the same size. Three quarters is greater than two quarters because three pieces are more than two.

Now compare one third and one sixth. These wholes are the same length, but the sixths are smaller pieces.

So one third is greater than one sixth. A bigger denominator does not always mean a bigger fraction.

Return to three quarters and one half. Split each half into two equal pieces. One half becomes two quarters.

Now the parts match. Three quarters is greater than two quarters, so three quarters is greater than one half.

A number line shows fraction size as distance from zero. Divide the distance from zero to one into four equal intervals.

One quarter is one interval from zero. One half is two intervals, and three quarters is three intervals.

Read from left to right to order them from smallest to largest: one quarter, one half, then three quarters.
