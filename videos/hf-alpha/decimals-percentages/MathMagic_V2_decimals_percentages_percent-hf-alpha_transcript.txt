What does a battery showing seventy-five percent tell you? Today we are exploring percent as a way of describing relative size. We will use one whole divided into one hundred equal parts and compare familiar percentages on the same scale.

Our learning goal is to explain percentages as parts of a whole divided into 100 equal parts.

Success criteria. I can explain what the percent symbol means. I can represent a familiar percentage using a hundred-part model. I can compare percentages by thinking about the same whole.

Percent means per hundred. One hundred percent is one complete whole. Fifty percent is half of that whole. Always identify the whole: a percentage describes a share of it, rather than a count by itself.

A battery at seventy-five percent represents seventy-five of one hundred equal parts of full capacity. It is more than half full but less than completely full.

The percentage bar shows the same relative share: three quarters of its complete length. The full bar always stands for the same full battery in this comparison.

Compare forty percent and sixty percent of the same whole. Imagine forty shaded cells and sixty shaded cells on equal hundred-grids.

Sixty percent is the greater share because sixty of the same-sized parts is more than forty. Both shares use a common scale of one hundred.

Which is closer to a complete whole: ninety percent or twenty-five percent?

Ninety percent is only ten of the hundred parts below the whole. Twenty-five percent is seventy-five parts below it. Ninety percent is closer.

Percent means out of one hundred equal parts. Identify the whole, use familiar benchmarks such as fifty and one hundred percent, and compare relative shares on the same scale.
