Can one amount have three different mathematical names?

Today we are connecting fractions, decimals and percentages.

We will use the same whole each time, rename shares in hundredths, and explain why the notation changes while the amount stays the same.

Our learning goal is to connect familiar fractions, decimals and percentages that represent the same amount.

Success criteria.

I can match familiar fractions to equivalent decimals and percentages.

I can use a visual model to explain why the forms are equivalent.

I can place equivalent representations at the same position on a number line.

A fraction describes equal parts, a decimal uses place-value parts, and a percentage uses hundredths.

Rename a familiar fraction in hundredths to connect all three.

Always keep the whole unchanged.

One half of a whole is fifty hundredths.

In decimal notation that is zero point five zero, or zero point five.

Fifty hundredths is fifty percent.

All three names identify the midpoint between zero and one, so the amount has not changed.

Three quarters is seventy-five hundredths.

In decimal notation it is zero point seven five.

Seventy-five hundredths is seventy-five percent.

One quarter would be twenty-five hundredths, or zero point two five, or twenty-five percent.

One fifth is how many tenths, and what decimal and percentage does it represent?

One fifth is two tenths, or twenty hundredths.

That gives zero point two and twenty percent.

Keep the same whole and connect through equal parts.

One half, one quarter, three quarters, one tenth and one fifth are useful benchmarks.

Equivalent representations locate the same amount on a number line.
