What operation fits the question: how much altogether?

First identify the whole: one cup.

Altogether tells us to combine the amounts, so choose addition.

A useful answer tells the amount and its unit: three quarters of a cup.

Compare it with the ingredients to check that nothing has disappeared or been counted twice.

A ribbon is two metres long.

We cut off three quarters of a metre.

Will the remaining length be more or less than one metre?

Now a ribbon is two metres long.

We cut off three quarters of a metre.

How much ribbon remains?

We want what remains, so subtract.

Two metres equals eight quarter-metres.

Eight quarters minus three quarters is five quarters.

Five quarters is one and one quarter.

One and one quarter metres remain.

On the number line, move back three quarter-steps from two.

The three quarters refers to one metre, not three quarters of the two-metre ribbon.

Naming the unit clearly prevents a very different calculation.

For a comparison, Ava walks three quarters of a kilometre.

Leo walks five eighths of a kilometre.

Who walks farther, and by how much?

Rename three quarters as six eighths.

Ava walks farther because six eighths is greater than five eighths.

Subtract five eighths from six eighths.

Ava walks one eighth of a kilometre farther.

Here is a two-step problem.

A jug holds one litre.

We pour out a quarter of a litre, then another half a litre.

How much remains?

A quarter plus a half is three quarters of a litre poured out.

Subtract three quarters from one whole litre.

One quarter of a litre remains.

Check: three quarters poured out plus one quarter remaining makes one whole litre.
