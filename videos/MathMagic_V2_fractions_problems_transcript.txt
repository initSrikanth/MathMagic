A recipe needs one half of a cup of oats and one quarter of a cup of nuts. How much goes into the bowl altogether? Today we are solving fraction problems by modelling the situation, choosing an operation, and checking whether our answer makes sense.

First identify the whole: one cup. Altogether tells us to combine the amounts, so choose addition.

Rename one half as two quarters. Two quarters plus one quarter makes three quarters.

Mia uses three quarters of a cup altogether. That is more than half a cup and less than one cup, so the answer makes sense.

Now a ribbon is two metres long. We cut off three quarters of a metre. How much ribbon remains?

We want what remains, so subtract. Two metres equals eight quarter-metres.

Eight quarters minus three quarters is five quarters. Five quarters is one and one quarter.

One and one quarter metres remain. On the number line, move back three quarter-steps from two.

For a comparison, Ava walks three quarters of a kilometre. Leo walks five eighths of a kilometre. Who walks farther, and by how much?

Rename three quarters as six eighths. Ava walks farther because six eighths is greater than five eighths.

Subtract five eighths from six eighths. Ava walks one eighth of a kilometre farther.

Here is a two-step problem. A jug holds one litre. We pour out a quarter of a litre, then another half a litre. How much remains?

A quarter plus a half is three quarters of a litre poured out. Subtract three quarters from one whole litre.

One quarter of a litre remains. Check: three quarters poured out plus one quarter remaining makes one whole litre.

You walk two thirds of a kilometre, then one sixth more. How far is that altogether?

Two thirds is four sixths. Adding one sixth gives five sixths of a kilometre.

Identify the whole, model the relationship, and choose an operation from the story. Rename to equal-sized parts, calculate carefully, and check the answer with its unit.
