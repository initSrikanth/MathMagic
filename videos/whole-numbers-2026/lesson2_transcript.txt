A number arrives at a costume party.

Sometimes it wears digits, sometimes words, and sometimes a long addition sentence.

Can we spot the same number beneath every disguise?

Let’s become number detectives.

Today, we’re learning to read, write and regroup a whole number without changing its value.

You’ll be able to match a number to its word form and expanded form.

You’ll be able to explain how zero holds an empty place.

You’ll be able to regroup thousands and hundreds to show the same amount.

How can one number have several correct representations?

We can write its digits, say it in words, or split it into place values.

All three describe the same quantity.

Let us represent four hundred and six thousand, two hundred and seventy five.

How would you describe this number without using its digits?

Notice the empty ten-thousands place before we unpack the number.

Start with the largest place.

The four means four hundred thousand.

The zero ten thousands contributes zero.

The six means six thousand.

Keep that zero as a placeholder when writing the standard number, so every other digit stays in the right place.

Now add the smaller places: two hundred, seventy and five.

The expanded form is four hundred thousand, plus six thousand, plus two hundred, plus seventy, plus five.

We can leave out the zero contribution without changing the total.

In words, say four hundred and six thousand, two hundred and seventy five.

Notice how the thousands group and the final three digits fit together.

The word form, standard form and expanded form all represent four hundred and six thousand, two hundred and seventy five.

Can we describe three thousand four hundred using only hundreds?

Predict how many hundreds would make the same amount.

We can also regroup a number.

Three thousand four hundred is three thousands and four hundreds.

It is also thirty four hundreds, because thirty four groups of one hundred make three thousand four hundred.

The grouping changes; the amount does not.

It is tempting to erase the zero when writing the number in digits.

But removing that placeholder shifts the other digits into different places.

Leave zero in the standard number, even when its zero contribution is omitted from expanded form.

What number is six hundred thousand plus twenty thousand plus four thousand plus three hundred plus eight?

The total is six hundred and twenty-four thousand, three hundred and eight.

There are zero tens.

For practice, combine six hundred thousand, twenty thousand, four thousand, three hundred and eight.

That gives six hundred and twenty four thousand, three hundred and eight.

There are no tens, but the zero still holds their place.

Remember: read, partition and regroup, while keeping the value unchanged.
