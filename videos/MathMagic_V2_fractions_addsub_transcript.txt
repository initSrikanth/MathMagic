How can we add pieces when the pieces have different names? Today we are adding and subtracting fractions. We will use equal-sized wholes and rename parts when we need to. What should happen to the size of the pieces when we combine them?

First, use matching denominators. Two sevenths plus three sevenths makes five sevenths.

We add the numerators and keep the denominator. We are still counting sevenths, not fourteenths.

For one half plus one quarter, the denominators are related: four is a multiple of two. Rename the half as two quarters.

Now add two quarters and one quarter. The result is three quarters.

Subtraction follows the same idea. For three quarters minus one eighth, rename three quarters as six eighths.

Take away one eighth. Five eighths remain. The denominator stays eight.

On a number line, begin at six eighths and move back one eighth. You land on five eighths.

Simplify when you can. One sixth plus three sixths is four sixths, which equals two thirds.

Mixed numbers can make another whole. One and four fifths plus two fifths is one and six fifths.

Regroup five fifths as one whole. The answer is two and one fifth.

For several fractions, rename them with a common denominator. One half plus one quarter minus one eighth becomes four eighths plus two eighths minus one eighth.

Four plus two minus one is five, so the result is five eighths.

Here is an extension: when neither denominator is a multiple of the other, shared multiples can help. Try one quarter plus one sixth.

Multiples of four include four, eight, twelve. Multiples of six include six, twelve, eighteen. Twelve is the smallest positive shared multiple.

One quarter is three twelfths. One sixth is two twelfths. Add to get five twelfths.

What is two thirds minus one sixth?

Two thirds is four sixths. Four sixths minus one sixth is three sixths, or one half.

Use the same-sized whole and same-sized parts. Rename fractions when needed, combine or remove parts, and check the size of the result. Shared-denominator methods beyond related denominators are extension work.
