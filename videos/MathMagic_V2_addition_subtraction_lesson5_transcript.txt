Does the word more always tell you to add? Today we are modelling addition and subtraction problems. We will identify the quantities, decide how they relate, and check both the number and the unit. The story, rather than one keyword, tells us what to do.

Our learning goal is to model everyday addition and subtraction problems and explain solutions.

Success criteria. I can identify the known quantities and the question. I can use a bar model to choose an operation. I can give an answer with its unit and check its size.

First understand what is known and what is being asked. A total combines parts; a difference compares amounts or finds what remains. A simple bar can show those relationships before you calculate.

A library has one thousand two hundred and forty-eight books and receives three hundred and seventy-five more. Both quantities are parts of the new total, so add.

The new total is one thousand six hundred and twenty-three books. It is greater than either part, which fits the story. A rough check gives about twelve hundred plus four hundred, or sixteen hundred.

A class needs nine hundred dollars and has saved six hundred and seventy-five dollars. How much more is needed? This asks for the gap, so subtract.

Nine hundred minus six hundred and seventy-five is two hundred and twenty-five dollars. Adding the gap to the savings returns the target, so the model and answer agree.

The word more does not always mean add. How much more is needed asks for a gap. Picture the savings as one part of the target, find the missing part, and add it back to check.

A school has eight hundred and twenty seats and five hundred and eighty-six are filled. How many are empty?

Two hundred and thirty-four seats are empty. Filled seats and empty seats combine to make all eight hundred and twenty seats.

Understand, model, calculate, and check. Choose an operation from the relationship between quantities. Give the answer with its unit and explain why its size makes sense. Now choose a strategy and explain why it works. Have a go at the lesson practice on MathMagic!
