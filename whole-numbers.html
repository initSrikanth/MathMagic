Understanding Place Value

A digit's value depends on where it sits in a number.

KEY IDEA

Moving one place to the left makes a digit's value 10 times greater. Moving one place to the right makes it 10 times smaller.

Worked example

In 472,631, what is the value of the digit 7?

7 is in the ten-thousands place → 70,000.

Think about it

In 55,000, why does the first 5 have ten times the value of the second 5?

Show explanation

The first 5 represents 50,000 and the second represents 5,000. 50,000 is 10 × 5,000.

Expanded Form & Representing Numbers

The same number can be represented in several useful ways.

KEY IDEA

Break a number into the value of each non-zero digit. This is called expanded form.

Worked example

406,275

400,000 + 6,000 + 200 + 70 + 5

Word form: four hundred and six thousand, two hundred and seventy-five.

Regrouping

3,400 can be thought of as 3 thousands + 4 hundreds, or 34 hundreds. The quantity has not changed.

Quick practice

Which number is 600,000 + 20,000 + 4,000 + 300 + 8?

Think about it

Why is there no “tens” part in the expanded form of 624,308?

Show explanation

The tens digit is 0, so its value is zero and it does not need to be written in expanded form.

PAGE 3

Comparing & Ordering Whole Numbers

Compare numbers from the largest place value first.

STEPS

1. Compare the leftmost digits.   2. If they are equal, move one place right.   3. Stop at the first difference.

Worked example

Which is greater: 456,782 or 456,728?

456,782 and 456,728 match through the hundreds. Compare tens: 8 > 2. Therefore 456,782 > 456,728.

Quick practice

Enter <, > or = : 309,950 ___ 310,005

Think about it

Sam says 89,999 is greater than 90,001 because 999 is greater than 001. What has Sam forgotten?

Show explanation

He must compare from the greatest place value. 90,001 has 9 ten-thousands while 89,999 has only 8, so 90,001 is greater.

PAGE 4

Decimal Place Value

The place-value system continues to the right of the decimal point.

KEY IDEA

Each place is 10 times the value of the place to its right. After ones come tenths, hundredths and thousandths.

Ones
Tenths
Hundredths
Thousandths

Worked example

In 4.726, the 7 represents 7 tenths = 0.7, the 2 represents 2 hundredths = 0.02, and the 6 represents 6 thousandths = 0.006.

4.726 = 4 + 0.7 + 0.02 + 0.006

Ten times as much

A tenth is 10 times a hundredth. A hundredth is 10 times a thousandth. Moving a digit one place left makes its value 10 times greater.

Quick practice

What is the value of the 5 in 3.458?

Think about it

Why is 0.5 greater than 0.05?

Show explanation

Five tenths is 10 times five hundredths. The digit is the same, but its place changes its value.

PAGE 5

Compare, Order & Locate Decimals

Use place value and number lines to decide where decimals belong.

STEPS

Compare the whole-number parts first. Then compare tenths, hundredths and thousandths from left to right. Zeros at the end can help line up the places.

Worked example

Compare 1.407 and 1.47.

Write 1.47 as 1.470. Ones and tenths match. At the hundredths place, 0 < 7, so 1.407 < 1.470.

Number line

Between 1.40 and 1.50, each hundredth has its own position. A number such as 1.437 sits between 1.43 and 1.44.

Ordering

Line up the decimal places: 2.053 < 2.305 < 2.350.

Quick practice

Enter <, > or = : 3.408 ___ 3.48

Think about it

Why is 4.7 equal to 4.700?

Show explanation

Adding zeros to the end of a decimal does not change its value. Seven tenths is the same amount as seven hundred thousandths.
