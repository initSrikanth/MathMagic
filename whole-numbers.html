Understanding Place Value

A digit's value depends on where it sits in a number.

KEY IDEA

Moving one place to the left makes a digit's value 10 times greater. Moving one place to the right makes it 10 times smaller.

Worked example

In 472,631, what is the value of the digit 7?

7 is in the ten-thousands place → 70,000.

Quick practice

In 583,214, what is the value of the digit 8?

Think about it

In 55,000, why does the first 5 have ten times the value of the second 5?

Show explanation

The first 5 represents 50,000 and the second represents 5,000. 50,000 is 10 × 5,000.

What makes the same digit worth ten times as much? Its position does. In our base ten system, ten ones make one ten, and ten tens make one hundred. Each place is ten times the place immediately to its right.

Look at four hundred and seventy two thousand, six hundred and thirty one. Put each digit into its own column, from hundred thousands down to ones. The seven is in the ten thousands column. Its value is seventy thousand, not seven thousand.

The two stands for two thousand, the six for six hundred, and the three for thirty. Reading the number in groups helps: four hundred and seventy two thousand, then six hundred and thirty one. Every digit has a job.

Now compare the two fives in fifty five thousand. The first five represents fifty thousand. The second represents five thousand. Fifty thousand is ten times five thousand, even though the digits look the same.

This relationship helps us multiply and divide whole numbers by ten. Thirty four times ten is three hundred and forty. Each digit takes a place worth ten times as much. Three hundred and forty divided by ten returns thirty four.

Try the page example: five hundred and eighty three thousand, two hundred and fourteen. The eight is in the ten thousands place, so it represents eighty thousand. Remember: name the place first, then work out the value. One place left means ten times as much.

Expanded Form & Representing Numbers

The same number can be represented in several useful ways.

KEY IDEA

Break a number into the value of each non-zero digit. This is called expanded form.

Worked example

406,275

400,000 + 6,000 + 200 + 70 + 5

Word form: four hundred and six thousand, two hundred and seventy-five.

Regrouping

3,400 can be thought of as 3 thousands + 4 hundreds, or 34 hundreds. The quantity has not changed.

Quick practice

Which number is 600,000 + 20,000 + 4,000 + 300 + 8?

Think about it

Why is there no “tens” part in the expanded form of 624,308?

Show explanation

The tens digit is 0, so its value is zero and it does not need to be written in expanded form.

MathMagic explainer: Expanded Form & Representing Numbers

Open the MathMagic lesson video ↗
Read the Expanded Form & Representing Numbers video transcript

How can one number have several correct representations? We can write its digits, say it in words, or split it into place values. All three describe the same quantity. Let us represent four hundred and six thousand, two hundred and seventy five.

Start with the largest place. The four means four hundred thousand. The zero ten thousands contributes zero. The six means six thousand. Keep that zero as a placeholder when writing the standard number, so every other digit stays in the right place.

Now add the smaller places: two hundred, seventy and five. The expanded form is four hundred thousand, plus six thousand, plus two hundred, plus seventy, plus five. We can leave out the zero contribution without changing the total.

In words, say four hundred and six thousand, two hundred and seventy five. Notice how the thousands group and the final three digits fit together. The word form, standard form and expanded form all represent four hundred and six thousand, two hundred and seventy five.

We can also regroup a number. Three thousand four hundred is three thousands and four hundreds. It is also thirty four hundreds, because thirty four groups of one hundred make three thousand four hundred. The grouping changes; the amount does not.

For practice, combine six hundred thousand, twenty thousand, four thousand, three hundred and eight. That gives six hundred and twenty four thousand, three hundred and eight. There are no tens, but the zero still holds their place. Remember: read, partition and regroup, while keeping the value unchanged.

Comparing & Ordering Whole Numbers

Compare numbers from the largest place value first.

STEPS

1. Compare the leftmost digits.   2. If they are equal, move one place right.   3. Stop at the first difference.

Worked example

Which is greater: 456,782 or 456,728?

456,782 and 456,728 match through the hundreds. Compare tens: 8 > 2. Therefore 456,782 > 456,728.

Quick practice

Enter <, > or = : 309,950 ___ 310,005

Think about it

Sam says 89,999 is greater than 90,001 because 999 is greater than 001. What has Sam forgotten?

Show explanation

He must compare from the greatest place value. 90,001 has 9 ten-thousands while 89,999 has only 8, so 90,001 is greater.

MathMagic explainer: Comparing & Ordering Whole Numbers

Open the MathMagic lesson video ↗
Read the Comparing & Ordering Whole Numbers video transcript

Which number is larger when most of the digits match? Compare from the greatest place value, not from the right. For whole numbers with no leading zeros, more digits means a larger number. If the lengths match, compare one column at a time.

Compare four hundred and fifty six thousand, seven hundred and eighty two, with four hundred and fifty six thousand, seven hundred and twenty eight. Their hundred thousands, ten thousands, thousands and hundreds match. We have not found a difference yet, so keep moving right.

At the tens place, eight is greater than two. Stop there. The first number is greater, so four hundred and fifty six thousand, seven hundred and eighty two is greater than four hundred and fifty six thousand, seven hundred and twenty eight. The ones cannot overturn that difference.

Now compare three hundred and nine thousand, nine hundred and fifty, with three hundred and ten thousand and five. The hundred thousands match. At ten thousands, zero is less than one. Therefore three hundred and nine thousand, nine hundred and fifty is smaller.

To order several numbers, repeat the same method. Eighty nine thousand, nine hundred and ninety nine comes before ninety thousand and one, which comes before ninety thousand and ten. Do not compare only the final three digits. The greatest differing place decides.

As a supporting estimation check, rounding eighty nine thousand, nine hundred and ninety nine to the nearest thousand gives ninety thousand. A rounded value is useful, but it does not replace an exact comparison. Recap: line up the places, start left, find the first difference, and explain your order.

Decimal Place Value

The place-value system continues to the right of the decimal point.

KEY IDEA

Each place is 10 times the value of the place to its right. After ones come tenths, hundredths and thousandths.

Ones
Tenths
Hundredths
Thousandths
4726

Worked example

In 4.726, the 7 represents 7 tenths = 0.7, the 2 represents 2 hundredths = 0.02, and the 6 represents 6 thousandths = 0.006.

4.726 = 4 + 0.7 + 0.02 + 0.006

Ten times as much

A tenth is 10 times a hundredth. A hundredth is 10 times a thousandth. Moving a digit one place left makes its value 10 times greater.

Quick practice

What is the value of the 5 in 3.458?

Think about it

Why is 0.5 greater than 0.05?

Show explanation

Five tenths is 10 times five hundredths. The digit is the same, but its place changes its value.

MathMagic explainer: Decimal Place Value

Open the MathMagic lesson video ↗
Read the Decimal Place Value video transcript

What happens when we split one whole into smaller equal parts? The same base ten pattern continues beyond the decimal point. After ones come tenths, hundredths and thousandths. Each place is one tenth of the value of the place immediately to its left.

Read four point seven two six. The four is four ones. The seven is seven tenths, written zero point seven. The two is two hundredths, written zero point zero two. The six is six thousandths, written zero point zero zero six.

Put the parts together. Four point seven two six equals four, plus zero point seven, plus zero point zero two, plus zero point zero zero six. The digits describe different sized parts, so their positions matter just as much as they do in whole numbers.

A tenth is ten hundredths. A hundredth is ten thousandths. Now compare zero point five with zero point zero five. Five tenths is ten times five hundredths, so zero point five is greater. The same digit does not mean the same value.

Think of a measuring scale marked in equal small steps. Thousandths let us describe positions more precisely than hundredths. This is about understanding place value, not just counting how many digits come after the point. Keep the whole and the size of each part clear.

Try the page example: three point four five eight. The five is in the hundredths place, so its value is zero point zero five. Remember: ones, tenths, hundredths, thousandths. Every step right divides the place value by ten; every step left makes it ten times as much.

Compare, Order & Locate Decimals

Use place value and number lines to decide where decimals belong.

STEPS

Compare the whole-number parts first. Then compare tenths, hundredths and thousandths from left to right. Zeros at the end can help line up the places.

Worked example

Compare 1.407 and 1.47.

Write 1.47 as 1.470. Ones and tenths match. At the hundredths place, 0 < 7, so 1.407 < 1.470.

Number line

Between 1.40 and 1.50, each hundredth has its own position. A number such as 1.437 sits between 1.43 and 1.44.

Ordering

Line up the decimal places: 2.053 < 2.305 < 2.350.

Quick practice

Enter <, > or = : 3.408 ___ 3.48

Think about it

Why is 4.7 equal to 4.700?

Show explanation

Adding zeros to the end of a decimal does not change its value. Seven tenths is the same amount as seven hundred thousandths.

Does a longer decimal always mean a bigger number? No. Compare place values instead. Start with the whole number parts, then tenths, hundredths and thousandths. Adding zeros at the end of a decimal can help line up the places without changing its value.

Compare one point four zero seven and one point four seven. Write the second as one point four seven zero. Ones and tenths match. At the hundredths place, zero is less than seven. So one point four zero seven is less than one point four seven zero.

For ordering, line up two point zero five three, two point three zero five, and two point three five zero. The first is smallest because its tenths digit is zero. The other two match in tenths, but zero hundredths is less than five hundredths.

Now locate a number on a line. From one point four zero to one point five zero, divide the interval into ten equal steps. Each step is one hundredth. One point four three and one point four four are neighbouring marks on this line.

To locate one point four three seven more precisely, zoom in between one point four three and one point four four. Divide that interval into ten equal steps. Each step is one thousandth. Count seven steps from one point four three zero to reach one point four three seven.

Finally, four point seven equals four point seven zero zero: both represent seven tenths after four wholes. Extra end zeros do not add any value. Remember: line up the places, compare from the left, and check the equal step size before locating a decimal.
